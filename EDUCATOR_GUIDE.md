# Educator guide

## Objectives, prerequisites and session
Target approximately age 9; suggested 10–15 minutes. No specialist prerequisite beyond reading short labels. Learners identify producers/consumers, trace a simple food-energy relationship, connect sunlight/water to producers and animal food, make a prediction, inspect deterministic steps and restore an unfamiliar struggling meadow.

## Meadow content
Coherent simplified meadow: grass and clover are producers; rabbit and grasshopper are herbivores; fox is the predator. Food-energy arrows point **food → consumer**. Sunlight supplies energy to producers, while water is a required nonliving resource in this model.

## Explicit deterministic model
All resource/population indicators are illustrative model values bounded to 0–100, never scientific measurements. Each step is deterministic:
1. Producer change is based on the lower of sunlight and water, representing a simple limiting-resource assumption; delta is bounded −6 to +4.
2. Both herbivores respond to the mean producer-food level; delta is bounded −5 to +3.
3. Fox responds to mean herbivore prey; delta is bounded −4 to +2.
4. Every value is clamped to 0–100.
The simplification intentionally omits reproduction delays, weather history, disease, migration, competition, decomposers and many real food-web links. It must not be used for ecological forecasting.

## Activities
1. **Build a habitat:** select organisms and check whether a producer/consumer/prey chain is present. Missing food sources get specific feedback.
2. **Predict & experiment:** use healthy, dry and shady starting scenarios; change light/water, record/skip a prediction, manually step or auto-run, pause and reset. Every step reports what changed and why.
3. **Restore balance:** unfamiliar cloudy-drought scenario. Learners diagnose resource limitation, change light/water and step repeatedly. Recovery only occurs through model calculations.

## Sources
1. **U.S. National Park Service — “Create Your Own Marine Food Web”**  
https://www.nps.gov/caco/planyourvisit/create-your-own-marine-food-web.htm  
Supports the core producer/consumer energy-flow idea: sunlight begins energy transfer, producers use sunlight to make food, and consumers obtain energy by consuming producers/other organisms.

2. **NOAA educational material — “Chapter 4: Food Relationships”**  
https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/42208/noaa_42208_DS1.pdf  
Supports ecosystems as interactions of living/nonliving components and explains that plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make food; it informs the lesson’s resource-to-producer relationship.

## Differentiation and offline follow-up
For lower load, begin with the healthy example and one manual step at a time. For extension, ask which omitted relationship could change the result. Offline, draw a meadow food web on paper, label arrows “energy from food to consumer,” circle nonliving conditions such as light/water, and predict one indirect effect of a drought.
